Consciousness Research -- Research Frameworks for Computational Phenomenology, Self-Model Theory, and Recursive Awareness Modeling

Theoretical foundations and research frameworks for applying multi-agent coordination, epistemic verification, and formal reasoning to consciousness studies, Theory of Mind modeling, and self-referential system analysis within the Prismatic Platform

Abstract

This document provides a comprehensive overview of the Prismatic Platform's consciousness research domain -- a collection of 25 theoretical frameworks designed to study the application of multi-agent systems, formal verification, and computational phenomenology to problems in consciousness studies, Theory of Mind (ToM) modeling, and self-referential system analysis. The domain spans five primary research areas: awareness and self-model theory, Theory of Mind and recursive belief, epistemic dynamics and attractor states, identity and coherence analysis, and consciousness measurement and comparison.

This domain occupies a unique philosophical position within the platform -- it studies the properties of self-referential systems using tools that are themselves computational systems. The platform's NABLA Infinity framework provides the epistemic infrastructure for modeling how systems can maintain models of their own states, while the contradiction preservation axiom acknowledges that self-reference inevitably produces paradoxes that must be navigated rather than eliminated.

Introduction

Context and Motivation

Consciousness research stands at the intersection of philosophy, neuroscience, psychology, and computer science. The "hard problem" of consciousness -- explaining why and how physical processes give rise to subjective experience -- remains one of the deepest unsolved problems in science and philosophy. While this platform cannot solve the hard problem, it can provide formal frameworks for studying computational aspects of consciousness-related phenomena: self-modeling, recursive awareness, identity coherence, and Theory of Mind.

The Prismatic Platform's consciousness research domain uses multi-agent architectures to study systems that maintain models of themselves and of other agents' mental states. The platform's event replay infrastructure enables deterministic reconstruction of "epistemic trajectories" -- the sequences of belief revisions that lead to particular self-model states -- providing unprecedented analytical access to the dynamics of self-referential reasoning.

Problem Definition

  1. Self-Reference and Paradox: Systems that model themselves inevitably encounter self-referential paradoxes (analogous to the liar's paradox). Computational frameworks must handle these paradoxes gracefully rather than entering infinite loops or producing contradictions.

  2. Recursive Depth Limits: Theory of Mind involves recursive belief modeling (A believes that B believes that A believes...). Understanding the computational limits and practical depth of recursive ToM is both theoretically and practically important.

  3. Consciousness Detection: If consciousness is a property that some systems possess, what are the measurable correlates? Different theories (IIT, GWT, Higher-Order Theories) propose different metrics that may produce contradictory assessments.

  4. Identity Over Time: How systems maintain coherent identity through change -- especially through changes to their own self-model -- is a fundamental question for both philosophy and AI development.

  5. Cross-System Comparison: Comparing consciousness-related properties across different architectures requires formal metrics that abstract from implementation details.
