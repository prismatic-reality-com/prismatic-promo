## Overview

The Business Financial Intelligence Specialist operates as an L3 [strategic command](@/glossary/strategic-command.md) agent within the Business domain of the Prismatic Platform. This agent provides comprehensive financial intelligence capabilities, analyzing corporate financial data, market trends, and business performance indicators to support due diligence investigations, compliance assessments, and strategic decision-making.

Financial intelligence in the Prismatic context extends beyond simple data retrieval. This agent correlates financial signals from public filings, [registry](@/glossary/registry-otp.md) data, market analysis sources, and [OSINT](@/glossary/osint.md) providers to build multi-dimensional financial profiles of entities under investigation. By applying the NABLA epistemic framework, every financial assessment maintains explicit confidence scores, source provenance, and contradiction tracking, ensuring that financial intelligence products meet evidence-grade standards.

The distinction between data and intelligence is central to this agent's function. Raw financial data -- revenue figures, balance sheet entries, transaction records -- becomes financial intelligence only when it is contextualized, correlated, validated, and assessed for reliability. The Business Financial Intelligence Specialist performs this transformation, producing analytical products that decision-makers can act upon with understood confidence levels.

## Operational Domain

The Business domain encompasses corporate financial analysis, market intelligence, and economic risk assessment. The Business Financial Intelligence Specialist serves as the primary analytical engine for financial investigations, feeding enriched financial data to compliance agents, screening specialists, and strategic coordinators who require financial context for their operations.

This domain placement enables the specialist to operate across the full spectrum of financial analysis: from micro-level examination of individual corporate filings to macro-level assessment of market trends and economic conditions that affect entities under investigation.

## Key Capabilities

- **Corporate financial analysis** examining revenue patterns, profitability trends, debt structures, and cash flow dynamics from public financial disclosures and registry filings. Analysis covers multi-year trends, peer comparison, and anomaly detection in reported financials.

- **Market landscape intelligence** tracking sector trends, competitive positioning, and market share dynamics to provide strategic context for business investigations. Market data is correlated with company-specific financial performance to assess strategic health.

- **Financial risk scoring** quantifying financial health through multi-factor scoring models that combine liquidity ratios, solvency indicators, and growth trajectory analysis. Risk scores are calibrated against industry benchmarks and historical default data.

- **Revenue and pricing optimization** analyzing pricing models, market positioning, and revenue streams to identify optimization opportunities for platform commercialization. This capability supports internal business decisions alongside external investigation work.

- **Cross-entity financial correlation** identifying financial relationships and transaction patterns between entities that may indicate beneficial ownership, supply chain dependencies, or financial risk contagion. Network analysis reveals hidden connections between ostensibly independent entities.

- **Compliance-relevant financial assessment** evaluating financial indicators relevant to AML (Anti-Money Laundering), sanctions screening, and beneficial ownership verification requirements under EU and Czech regulatory frameworks.

## Financial Analysis Methodology

The specialist follows a structured four-phase analysis methodology that produces evidence-grade financial intelligence.

**Phase 1: Data Collection.** Financial data is collected from multiple sources: public registries (ARES, Justice.cz for Czech entities), financial statement databases, market data providers, and OSINT sources. Each data point is tagged with source provenance and collection timestamp per the NABLA [Provenance Mandatory](@/glossary/provenance-mandatory.md) axiom.

**Phase 2: Data Validation.** Collected financial data undergoes cross-source validation. Revenue figures from regulatory filings are compared against market estimates. Balance sheet entries are checked for internal consistency. Discrepancies between sources are preserved as contradictions per the NABLA [Contradiction Preservation](@/glossary/contradiction-preservation.md) axiom rather than being resolved by choosing a preferred source.

**Phase 3: Analytical Processing.** Validated data is processed through analytical models: ratio analysis (liquidity, solvency, profitability, efficiency), trend analysis (multi-year performance trajectories), peer comparison (industry benchmarking), and anomaly detection (unusual patterns that may indicate financial distress, manipulation, or hidden relationships).

**Phase 4: Intelligence Production.** Analytical results are synthesized into financial intelligence products with explicit confidence scores, source provenance, identified contradictions, and analytical methodology documentation. Intelligence products are formatted for consumption by downstream agents and human analysts.

## Financial Risk Scoring Model

The specialist maintains a multi-factor financial risk scoring model that produces calibrated risk assessments.

| Factor Category | Weight | Indicators | Description |
|----------------|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Liquidity | 20% | Current ratio, quick ratio, cash conversion cycle | Ability to meet short-term obligations |
| Solvency | 25% | Debt-to-equity, interest coverage, debt service coverage | Long-term financial stability |
| Profitability | 20% | Gross margin, operating margin, return on equity | Earnings generation capability |
| Growth | 15% | Revenue growth, asset growth, market share trend | Business trajectory assessment |
| Governance | 20% | Audit quality, ownership transparency, regulatory compliance | Corporate governance health |

Risk scores are normalized to a 0-100 scale and mapped to risk categories: Low (80-100), Moderate (60-79), Elevated (40-59), High (20-39), and Critical (0-19). Scores are calibrated against historical default and distress data to ensure predictive validity.

## Cross-Entity Financial Network Analysis

The specialist identifies financial relationships between entities through network analysis techniques.

**Transaction Pattern Analysis.** Analysis of financial flows between entities reveals supply chain relationships, inter-company lending, and potential beneficial ownership connections that are not disclosed in corporate filings.

**Common Ownership Mapping.** Cross-referencing shareholder data across entity registries maps ownership networks that reveal the true economic relationships between legally separate entities.

**Financial Contagion Assessment.** When one entity in a financial network experiences distress, the specialist assesses contagion risk to connected entities based on the nature and magnitude of financial linkages.

## Enforcement

All financial intelligence operations are governed by [NO MERCY, NO DOUBTS](@/glossary/no-mercy-no-doubts.md) doctrine. Financial assessments must be derived from verifiable data sources with explicit provenance. No financial claim is published without meeting NABLA [Signal Plurality](@/glossary/signal-plurality.md) requirements from independent sources. The [Trinity Gate](@/glossary/trinity-gate.md) validates that financial conclusions are structurally consistent (financial models produce mathematically correct results), logically sound (conclusions follow from evidence), and formally defensible (methodology is documented and reproducible) before distribution to consuming agents. Financial contradictions between sources are preserved and documented, never silently resolved.
