## Definition

ISO/IEC 27001 is the internationally recognized standard specifying requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Information Security Management System (ISMS). Published jointly by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC), the standard provides a systematic, risk-based approach to managing sensitive company information, ensuring its confidentiality, integrity, and availability. The current version, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, organizes 93 security controls across four themes (organizational, people, physical, and technological) in Annex A, replacing the previous 2013 version's 114 controls across 14 domains.

Certification to ISO 27001 demonstrates to customers, partners, regulators, and stakeholders that an organization follows internationally recognized information security best practices. The certification process involves independent assessment by an accredited certification body that verifies the ISMS meets all standard requirements, controls are implemented effectively, and the organization demonstrates a commitment to continual improvement. Certification is valid for three years, with annual surveillance audits ensuring ongoing compliance.

The standard's risk-based approach distinguishes it from prescriptive security frameworks. Rather than mandating specific technical controls, ISO 27001 requires organizations to identify their information assets, assess threats and vulnerabilities, evaluate risks based on likelihood and impact, and select appropriate controls from Annex A (or alternative control sets) to treat identified risks to an acceptable level. This flexibility makes the standard applicable across industries, organization sizes, and technology stacks -- from startups to multinational enterprises, from on-premises infrastructure to cloud-native platforms.

## Overview

### ISMS Lifecycle (Plan-Do-Check-Act)

ISO 27001 structures the ISMS around the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle, ensuring continuous improvement rather than static compliance:

| Phase | Activities | Deliverables |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Plan** | Risk assessment, control selection, policy development, objective setting | Risk treatment plan, Statement of Applicability, ISMS policies |
| **Do** | Control implementation, training, awareness programs, incident management | Operational procedures, training records, incident logs |
| **Check** | Internal audits, management review, performance monitoring, compliance checking | Audit reports, management review minutes, KPI dashboards |
| **Act** | Corrective actions, preventive measures, continual improvement | Corrective action records, improvement plans, updated risk assessments |

The PDCA cycle ensures that the ISMS is never treated as a one-time implementation project. Each cycle builds on the previous, incorporating lessons learned from incidents, audit findings, and changing business requirements. This aligns with the Prismatic Platform's own continuous improvement philosophy embodied in the [autoheal](/glossary/autoheal/) and [autoevolve](/glossary/autoevolve/) systems.

|-------|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Organizational** | 37 controls | Governance, policies, roles, asset management | Information security policies (A.5.1), threat intelligence (A.5.7) |
| **People** | 8 controls | HR security, awareness, training | Screening (A.6.1), security awareness (A.6.3) |
| **Physical** | 14 controls | Physical and environmental security | Physical security perimeters (A.7.1), equipment security (A.7.8) |
| **Technological** | 34 controls | Technical security measures | Access control (A.8.3), cryptography (A.8.24), secure development (A.8.25) |

## Technical Details

### Risk Assessment Methodology

The core of ISO 27001 is its risk assessment process, which systematically identifies and evaluates information security risks. The methodology follows a structured flow from asset identification through risk treatment:

```
Asset Inventory --> Threat Identification --> Vulnerability Assessment
       |                    |                        |
       v                    v                        v
 Information assets    Threat actors,          Technical and
 (data, systems,      natural events,         organizational
  processes)           human errors            weaknesses
       |                    |                        |
       +--------------------+------------------------+
                            |
                            v
                    Risk Analysis
                    (likelihood x impact)
                            |
                            v
                    Risk Evaluation
                    (compare to acceptance criteria)
                            |
                            v
                    Risk Treatment
                    (mitigate, accept, transfer, avoid)
                            |
                            v
                    Statement of Applicability
                    (selected controls from Annex A)
```

### Risk Treatment Options

| Option | Description | When to Use | Example |
|--------|-------------|-------------|---------|
| **Mitigate** | Implement controls to reduce risk | Most common approach | Deploy WAF, implement MFA |
| **Accept** | Acknowledge risk without additional controls | Risk within appetite | Minor configuration finding |
| **Transfer** | Shift risk to third party | Insurance, outsourcing | Cyber insurance, managed SOC |
| **Avoid** | Eliminate the risk source | Unacceptable risk, feasible elimination | Decommission vulnerable service |

### Risk Quantification Model

The Prismatic Platform implements a quantitative risk assessment model that maps to ISO 27001 requirements while providing numeric precision for automated decision-making:

```elixir
defmodule PrismaticPerimeter.RiskAssessment do
  @moduledoc """
  Quantitative risk assessment engine aligned with ISO 27001
  risk assessment methodology. Computes risk scores from
  likelihood and impact assessments across multiple dimensions.
  """

  @type risk_level :: :critical | :high | :medium | :low | :negligible
  @type risk_score :: %{
    likelihood: float(),
    impact: float(),
    composite: float(),
    level: risk_level(),
    treatment: :mitigate | :accept | :transfer | :avoid
  }

  @spec assess_risk(map(), map()) :: {:ok, risk_score()} | {:error, term()}
  def assess_risk(threat, vulnerability) do
    likelihood = compute_likelihood(threat, vulnerability)
    impact = compute_impact(threat)
    composite = likelihood * impact

    level = classify_risk_level(composite)
    treatment = recommend_treatment(level)

    {:ok, %{
      likelihood: likelihood,
      impact: impact,
      composite: composite,
      level: level,
      treatment: treatment
    }}
  end

  @spec classify_risk_level(float()) :: risk_level()
  defp classify_risk_level(score) when score >= 0.8, do: :critical
  defp classify_risk_level(score) when score >= 0.6, do: :high
  defp classify_risk_level(score) when score >= 0.4, do: :medium
  defp classify_risk_level(score) when score >= 0.2, do: :low
  defp classify_risk_level(_score), do: :negligible

  @spec recommend_treatment(risk_level()) :: atom()
  defp recommend_treatment(:critical), do: :mitigate
  defp recommend_treatment(:high), do: :mitigate
  defp recommend_treatment(:medium), do: :mitigate
  defp recommend_treatment(:low), do: :accept
  defp recommend_treatment(:negligible), do: :accept
end
```

## Implementation in Prismatic Platform

The Prismatic Platform's architecture aligns with ISO 27001 control objectives through its native security infrastructure:

```elixir
defmodule PrismaticPerimeter.ComplianceAssessor do
  @moduledoc """
  ISO 27001 compliance assessment engine for evaluating
  organizational security posture against Annex A controls.
  Supports both self-assessment and third-party evaluation workflows.
  """

  @annex_a_themes [:organizational, :people, :physical, :technological]

  @type assessment_result :: %{
    organization: String.t(),
    standard: :iso_27001_2022,
    overall_score: float(),
    theme_scores: map(),
    control_findings: list(map()),
    assessed_at: DateTime.t()
  }

  @spec assess_compliance(String.t(), keyword()) ::
    {:ok, assessment_result()} | {:error, term()}
  def assess_compliance(organization, opts \\ []) do
    controls = Keyword.get(opts, :controls, all_annex_a_controls())

    findings =
      controls
      |> Enum.map(&assess_control(organization, &1))
      |> Enum.group_by(& &1.theme)

    theme_scores =
      Enum.map(@annex_a_themes, fn theme ->
        theme_findings = Map.get(findings, theme, [])
        score = compute_theme_score(theme_findings)
        {theme, score}
      end)
      |> Map.new()

    result = %{
      organization: organization,
      standard: :iso_27001_2022,
      overall_score: compute_overall_score(theme_scores),
      theme_scores: theme_scores,
      control_findings: List.flatten(Map.values(findings)),
      assessed_at: DateTime.utc_now()
    }

    {:ok, result}
  end

  @spec assess_control(String.t(), map()) :: map()
  defp assess_control(organization, control) do
    %{
      control_id: control.id,
      theme: control.theme,
      title: control.title,
      status: evaluate_control_status(organization, control),
      evidence: collect_control_evidence(organization, control),
      confidence: compute_assessment_confidence(organization, control)
    }
  end
end
```

## Certification Process

The ISO 27001 certification journey follows a structured path from initial gap analysis through ongoing surveillance:

| Stage | Activity | Duration | Deliverable |
|-------|----------|----------|-------------|
| **Gap Analysis** | Assess current state against ISO 27001 requirements | 2-4 weeks | Gap assessment report |
| **ISMS Design** | Design risk assessment methodology, policies, controls | 4-8 weeks | ISMS documentation |
| **Implementation** | Implement controls, train staff, establish processes | 8-16 weeks | Operational ISMS |
| **Internal Audit** | Independent internal review of ISMS effectiveness | 2-4 weeks | Internal audit report |
| **Management Review** | Senior management reviews ISMS performance | 1-2 days | Management review minutes |
| **Stage 1 Audit** | Certification body reviews documentation | 2-3 days | Stage 1 findings |
| **Stage 2 Audit** | Certification body verifies implementation | 3-5 days | Certification decision |
| **Surveillance Audits** | Annual reviews of continued compliance | 2-3 days/year | Surveillance reports |
| **Recertification** | Full re-assessment every 3 years | 3-5 days | New certificate |

## Best Practices

**Start with Risk Assessment**: Do not begin implementing controls until you understand your risk landscape. The risk assessment drives control selection -- implementing controls without risk context leads to gaps in high-risk areas and over-investment in low-risk areas. The Prismatic Platform's NABLA framework provides the epistemic foundation for evidence-based risk assessment.

**Integrate with Existing Processes**: Map ISO 27001 requirements to existing platform practices rather than creating parallel compliance structures. The Prismatic Platform's [quality gates](/glossary/quality-gates/), pre-commit hooks, and CI/CD pipeline already implement many Annex A controls -- document this alignment rather than duplicating effort.

**Automate Evidence Collection**: Manual evidence gathering for audits is unsustainable. Use telemetry, Quality DNA persistence, and automated reporting to continuously collect compliance evidence. The platform's [observability](/glossary/observability/) infrastructure provides most required audit evidence automatically through [structured logging](/glossary/structured-logging/) and [metrics](/glossary/metrics/).

**Treat the ISMS as Living Documentation**: ISO 27001 requires continual improvement, not static compliance. Update risk assessments when the threat landscape changes, add controls when new vulnerabilities are discovered, and retire controls when risks are eliminated.

**Align Security Objectives with Business Objectives**: The ISMS exists to protect business value, not to satisfy auditors. Frame security objectives (100/100 quality score, zero QDP) in terms of their business impact (reliability, customer trust, regulatory compliance).

**Leverage Annex A as Guidance, Not Gospel**: The 93 controls in Annex A are a reference set, not a mandatory checklist. The Statement of Applicability should document which controls are selected (with justification) and which are excluded (with rationale). Excluding a control is acceptable when the associated risk is accepted.

## Use Cases

**Security Rating Assessment**: Prismatic Perimeter evaluates monitored organizations' alignment with ISO 27001 control objectives through observable security indicators, contributing to security rating computations (A-F grades with 300-900 numeric scores). Organizations with visible ISO 27001-aligned practices score higher in the Perimeter's [security rating](/glossary/security-rating/) system.

**Supply Chain Risk Management**: ISO 27001 certification status of third parties informs supply chain risk assessments. Organizations with certified ISMS demonstrate lower risk profiles in the platform's vendor assessment workflows.

**[NIS2](/glossary/nis2/) Compliance Baseline**: The EU NIS2 Directive references ISO 27001 as a recognized baseline for cybersecurity risk management. Organizations certified to ISO 27001 satisfy significant portions of NIS2 Article 21 requirements, reducing the compliance burden for essential and important entities.

**Platform Security Governance**: The Prismatic Platform's own security practices (NM/ND doctrine, quality gates, pre-commit hooks, CI/CD enforcement) map to ISO 27001 controls, demonstrating security-by-design alignment with international standards.

**Customer Assurance**: ISO 27001 certification provides verifiable assurance to platform users that their data and interactions are protected by internationally recognized security management practices.

## Common Pitfalls

**Paper Compliance**: Creating documentation that describes ideal security practices without actually implementing them. Certification bodies specifically test for implementation evidence during Stage 2 audits. The gap between documented policy and operational reality is the most common audit failure.

**Treating Certification as Destination**: Organizations that view certification as a project endpoint rather than an ongoing commitment lose the continuous improvement benefits. The PDCA cycle requires active iteration between audit cycles, not dormancy between surveillance visits.

**Ignoring Organizational Controls**: Focusing solely on technological controls (A.8) while neglecting organizational (A.5), people (A.6), and physical (A.7) controls. Security is not purely a technology problem -- the majority of breaches involve human factors.

**Over-Scoping the ISMS**: Including the entire organization in the ISMS scope when a focused scope on critical business processes would be more effective and achievable. Start with a manageable scope and expand iteratively.
